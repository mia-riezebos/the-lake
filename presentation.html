Three things that surprised me

  1. A limit on boats adapts by itself.

    Each boat catches less from a thinner lake, so a fixed boat count is quietly self-correcting. Only a limit counted in fish can be wrong for the year. That's the Grand Banks story.

  2. A ruinous fine works perfectly, if anyone will impose it.

    In my first model, harsh fines beat graduated ones, because a fine cost nobody anything. Graduated sanctions win once neighbours hesitate to ruin a neighbour. Valencia's 15th-century fine books agree: two-thirds of those fined in a year were fined only once.

  3. Hardin's lake doesn't die. It runs thin.

    Open access crashes once in the opening rush, then settles at 200 fish and about half the catch, for good. The tragedy isn't extinction. It's a lake that feeds half as many people for no reason.
