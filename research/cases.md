# The Lake: research notes and case copy

Fact-check and real-world text for `DESIGN.md`. Bracketed tags like **[O90]** point to the source list at the bottom. Where a claim rests on a secondary source or a search snippet instead of the primary text, it says so.

## 1. The eight principles

Ostrom's 1990 wording comes from Table 3.1 of *Governing the Commons* **[O90]**. Ostrom adopted the refined wording (Cox et al. split 1, 2 and 4 into A/B) in her Nobel lecture **[O09]**, and it comes from **[C10]**, Table 4. The **UI label / subtitle** column is mine and stays within 12 words.

| # | Ostrom 1990 | Refined (Ostrom 2009 / Cox et al. 2010) | UI label / subtitle |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Clearly defined boundaries.** "Individuals or households who have rights to withdraw resource units from the CPR must be clearly defined, as must the boundaries of the CPR itself." | **1A User boundaries:** "Clear and locally understood boundaries between legitimate users and nonusers are present." **1B Resource boundaries:** "Clear boundaries that separate a specific common-pool resource from a larger social-ecological system are present." | **Clear boundaries**: Only members fish here; outsiders can't just row in. |
| 2 | **Congruence between appropriation and provision rules and local conditions.** "Appropriation rules restricting time, place, technology, and/or quantity of resource units are related to local conditions and to provision rules requiring labor, material, and/or money." | **2A Congruence with local conditions:** "Appropriation and provision rules are congruent with local social and environmental conditions." **2B Appropriation and provision:** "Appropriation rules are congruent with provision rules; the distribution of costs is proportional to the distribution of benefits." | **Rules fit the lake**: Catch limits follow what this lake can actually give. |
| 3 | **Collective-choice arrangements.** "Most individuals affected by the operational rules can participate in modifying the operational rules." | "Most individuals affected by a resource regime are authorized to participate in making and modifying its rules." | **Fishers make the rules**: The people bound by the rules help write them. |
| 4 | **Monitoring.** "Monitors, who actively audit CPR conditions and appropriator behavior, are accountable to the appropriators or are the appropriators." | **4A Monitoring users:** "Individuals who are accountable to or are the users monitor the appropriation and provision levels of the users." **4B Monitoring the resource:** "Individuals who are accountable to or are the users monitor the condition of the resource." | **Monitoring**: Fishers, or people they answer to, watch catches and stock. |
| 5 | **Graduated sanctions.** "Appropriators who violate operational rules are likely to be assessed graduated sanctions (depending on the seriousness and context of the offense) by other appropriators, by officials accountable to these appropriators, or by both." | "Sanctions for rule violations start very low but become stronger if a user repeatedly violates a rule." | **Graduated sanctions**: First a warning, then penalties that grow with each repeat. |
| 6 | **Conflict-resolution mechanisms.** "Appropriators and their officials have rapid access to low-cost local arenas to resolve conflicts among appropriators or between appropriators and officials." | "Rapid, low-cost, local arenas exist for resolving conflicts among users or with officials." | **Conflict resolution**: A quick, cheap, local place to settle disputes. |
| 7 | **Minimal recognition of rights to organize.** "The rights of appropriators to devise their own institutions are not challenged by external governmental authorities." | "The rights of local users to make their own rules are recognized by the government." | **Right to organise**: Outside authorities respect the rules fishers made. |
| 8 | **Nested enterprises** (Ostrom lists this one only "for CPRs that are parts of larger systems"). "Appropriation, provision, monitoring, enforcement, conflict resolution, and governance activities are organized in multiple layers of nested enterprises." | "When a common-pool resource is closely connected to a larger social-ecological system, governance activities are organized in multiple nested layers." | **Nested governance**: Lake rules link up with the wider watershed's. |

How Ostrom framed them, worth keeping in the copy:
- 1990: "This list of design principles is still quite speculative. I am not yet willing to argue that these design principles are necessary conditions…" **[O90, ch. 3]**
- 2009: "The term 'design principle' has confused many readers. Perhaps I should have used the term 'best practices'." She adds that she "did not mean that the fishers, irrigators, pastoralists, and others overtly had these principles in their minds." **[O09, p. 422 and fn. 5]**

## 2. Hardin

**Core claim (one sentence):** Hardin argued that when a resource is open to all, each user gains the full benefit of taking more while bearing only a fraction of the cost, so rational users overuse it until it is ruined: "Freedom in a commons brings ruin to all." **[H68]**

Common misreadings, with sources:
- **He described open access, not common property.** His model is "a pasture open to all" **[H68]**. Thirty years later he wrote: "the weightiest mistake in my synthesizing paper was the omission of the modifying adjective 'unmanaged'… with an unmanaged commons… ruin is inevitable." **[H98]** Feeny, Berkes, McCay & Acheson (1990) showed that he conflated open access with communal property, and concluded that private, state and communal property are all potentially viable **[F90]**. Even in 1998 Hardin named only "socialism or the privatism of free enterprise" as managed options **[H98]**. Community self-governance, Ostrom's whole subject, appears in neither paper.
- **The essay was mainly about human population, not pastures or fish.** The commons parable serves his argument that "Freedom to breed will bring ruin to all." **[H68]**
- **He did not argue that only privatisation works.** His stated remedy was "mutual coercion, mutually agreed upon by the majority of the people affected" **[H68]**. That is close to Ostrom's principles 3 and 5 combined, which is worth one line of copy.
- **The historical commons he invoked were regulated.** Cox (1985), "No Tragedy of the Commons," argues that English common pastures were governed by rules such as stinting **[Cx85]**. I verified only the citation, not the full text, so cite it as an argument rather than as settled history.
- Ostrom on the reception: Hardin's portrayal "has been widely accepted since it was consistent with the prediction of no cooperation in a Prisoner's Dilemma" **[O09]**.

## 3. Cases (each ≤ 60 words)

### Alanya, Turkey: inshore fishery rotation
**Principles:** 1, 2A, 3, 4, 6, 7

> In the early 1970s, Alanya's roughly 100 inshore fishers were fighting, sometimes violently, over the best spots. After a decade of trial and error, they named every usable site, drew lots each September, and rotated daily: east until January, west after. Cheaters were seen by the fisher whose turn it was. Disputes were settled at the coffeehouse.

Sources: **[O90, ch. 1 and 3]**, which summarises **[B86]**. The fish migrate east to west from September to January and reverse from January to May, so the rotation gives everyone equal chances. The list is signed by each fisher and deposited with the mayor and gendarme. National law gave cooperatives jurisdiction over "local arrangements", which is principle 7.
- **Contrast for the UI:** nearby Bodrum and the Bay of Izmir "failed to establish effective rule systems" and "suffer severe problems of overcrowding and rent dissipation" **[O90, ch. 5]**.
- **Caveats:** Alanya is not centuries old; the rules date from the 1970s–80s. It is a site-allocation system, not a catch quota. Ostrom notes "no signs of overcapitalization", but the system does not cap total catch. A web snippet said the system ended by about 2005 because of tourism; I could not verify that and left it out.

### Törbel, Switzerland: alpine meadows
**Principles:** 1, 2A, 3, 4, 5

> Törbel, Switzerland, has written records of its alpine commons dating back to 1224; villagers formally organised in 1483. A 1517 rule, still enforced when Netting studied it, says no one may summer more cows on the alp than they can feed through winter. Cows are counted on arrival because cheese is shared per cow. An official fines violators, keeping half.

Source: **[O90, ch. 3]**, which quotes Netting (1976) and *Balancing on an Alp* (1981). Outsiders who bought land in Törbel gained no communal rights (principle 1). The cow count happens anyway because cheese is shared per cow, so monitoring costs almost nothing (principle 4).

### Valencia, Spain: huerta irrigation and the Tribunal de las Aguas
**Principles:** 2A, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8

> Valencia's huerta farmers have met to set their canal rules since at least 1435. Each canal's farmers elect a syndic, who must farm land on it. The Water Tribunal meets Thursday mornings outside the cathedral, without lawyers, and rules on the spot. Fifteenth-century fine books from nearby Castellón show two-thirds of offenders were never fined again.

Source: **[O90, ch. 3]**, drawing on Maass & Anderson and Glick (1970). Castellón recorded 441 fines in 1443 and 499 in 1486; "two-thirds of those fined in a year were 'one-time offenders'". Guards brought about two-thirds of the cases. After the court session the syndics meet as a second, cross-canal coordinating tribunal (principle 8). Drought declarations switch the allocation rules (principle 2A).
- UNESCO inscribed the tribunal on its intangible heritage list in 2009 and describes it as "eight elected administrators" who settle disputes "orally in a swift, transparent and impartial manner" **[U09]**. Ostrom (1990) says seven canals' syndics. The membership has changed over time, or the two count differently; if a number appears in the UI, use UNESCO's.

### Maine, USA: lobster "harbor gangs"
**Principles:** 1, 5 (informal), 7, 8

> To fish lobster in Maine you need a state licence and acceptance by your harbour's "gang", and then you fish only its territory. Gangs deter intruders with threats and, in extreme cases, destroyed gear. Since 1995, elected zone councils have set their own trap limits, which the state legislature had failed to pass.

Sources: **[A01]**, citing *The Lobster Gangs of Maine* **[A88]**. The state tolerates the territorial system "as long as violence and destruction of property are kept to a minimum". The 1995 Zone Management Law created zone councils of licence holders who propose trap limits, fishing times and, from 1999, limited entry; that is principles 7 and 8. Acheson concludes that fishermen "have been able to generate rules to constrain their own exploitive effort, which the legislature was unable to do."
- **Caveats:** The sanction escalation is informal, and the extreme step is vigilante gear destruction, so it is not a clean example of principle 5. Record landings since the late 1980s are real, but Acheson himself says "there is no agreement about the cause". Do not claim the gangs caused the boom.

### Grand Banks / northern cod, Canada: collapse
**Principles missing:** 1, 2A/4B, 3 (my mapping, explained below)

> Newfoundland's northern cod was not an unmanaged commons: Canada's federal government set the quotas. Its assessments, built on commercial catch rates, overestimated the stock; foreign fleets fished just outside Canada's 200-mile zone; inshore fishers' warnings were discounted. Overfishing collapsed the stock, and a moratorium on 2 July 1992 cost about 30,000 jobs. Commercial fishing reopened only in 2024.

Sources:
- Overexploitation, not the environment, caused the collapse: **[HM94]**. By 1993 six Canadian cod populations had collapsed far enough to bring moratoria, and the authors could not detect a recruitment failure: **[M97]**.
- Assessment and catch-rate overestimation: **[WM96]**. I verified the title; the lesson summarised here comes from a search snippet of the abstract.
- Foreign overfishing of the straddling stock on the "Nose and Tail" of the Banks, plus TACs set too high and discarding: House of Commons Fisheries Committee (2005) **[HoC05]**. The full text returned 403 to me, so this rests on a search snippet; verify it before quoting.
- Inshore knowledge disregarded: Finlayson (1994), *Fishing for Truth*, which I know only through secondary summaries. Treat it as plausible but unverified at primary level.
- Date and jobs: 2 July 1992 and about 30,000 people **[CBC24]**. Britannica splits the figure into about 19,000 direct and 20,000 indirect jobs, so "about 30,000" is the conservative headline.
- 2024 reopening with an 18,000 t TAC, after the stock left the "critical zone": **[CBC24]**.
- **The principle mapping is my interpretation.** None of these sources frame the collapse in Ostrom's terms. The honest framing: a top-down regime without user boundaries that matched the stock (1), a measured stock that matched the real one (2A/4B), or a voice for inshore users (3) failed too. That fits Ostrom's warning against panaceas **[O07]**, because state management is not automatically the fix either.

## 4. Model sanity

### What the meta-analyses say about which principles matter
**Cox, Arnold & Villamayor-Tomás 2010** **[C10]** coded 91 studies and 77 cases. Every principle was significant at p < 0.05 except principle 8, which reached p = 0.065. Their own strength bands are: ratio > 10 = very strong, 5–10 = strong, 2–5 = moderate.

| Principle (as coded in their Table 3) | Supportive : unsupportive ratio | φ |
|---|---|---|
| 4B monitors accountable to, or are, the users | **11.7** | **0.79** |
| 2B benefits proportional to inputs | **10.0** | 0.63 |
| 1A user boundaries | 5.3 | 0.69 |
| 2A rules fit local conditions | 5.0 | 0.74 |
| 4A monitors present | 3.8 | 0.59 |
| 6 conflict resolution | 3.2 | 0.43 |
| 3 collective choice | 2.8 | 0.47 |
| 5 graduated sanctions | 2.3 | 0.40 |
| 1B resource boundaries | 2.2 | 0.31 |
| 7 right to organise | 2.2 | 0.31 |
| 8 nested enterprises | 2.0 | 0.26 (p = 0.065) |

Note: in this analysis 4B meant *who* monitors (accountability). In their reformulation, 4B means monitoring the *resource*. The ratios above use the analysis coding.

**Baggio et al. 2016** **[Bg16]** ran a QCA on 69 cases: "No DP, alone, leads to successful outcomes (i.e. no single DP is sufficient and necessary for success)". More principles present means higher odds of success, and "the absence of DPs 2A, 2B, 4B and 5 seems to increase the likelihood of non-success." They also found that "clearly defined social boundaries are important when the natural infrastructure is highly mobile", and fish are mobile. Their fishery subsample is small and mostly missing data, which they flag themselves.

**Gutiérrez, Hilborn & Defeo 2011** **[G11]** studied 130 co-managed fisheries. Success tracked community leaders, social cohesion, catch shares and community protected areas; leadership was the strongest factor. Taken from the Nature editor summary and the abstract snippet.

**Monitoring in the field.** Across 178 forest user groups, monitoring level correlated strongly with forest condition, even controlling for formal organisation, dependence and social capital (Gibson, Williams & Ostrom 2005, reported in **[O09]**).

**Lab and field experiments** **[OWG92]**, as reported in **[O09]**:
- With no communication, subjects over-harvest, doing worse than even the Nash prediction.
- Face-to-face communication alone ("cheap talk") greatly raises returns.
- Sanctions imposed from outside the group are over-used and *lower* net returns.
- Groups that communicate *and choose their own* sanctioning system reach about 90% of optimal, the highest returns in any of these experiments.
- Colombian villagers under an externally imposed limit with a 1/16 inspection chance per round harvested *more* than villagers who only talked: regulation "crowded out" cooperation (Cardenas, Stranlund & Willis 2000, in **[O09]**).

Answer to the brief's question: the best-supported pieces are **accountable monitoring (4B), proportionality (2B), user boundaries (1A) and rules fitted to local conditions (2A)**. Graduated sanctions on their own are only moderately supported. "Monitoring + graduated sanctions matter most" is half right: monitoring yes, graduated sanctions less so.

### Biology parameters
- **r = 0.4 is defensible.** FishBase gives Atlantic cod a prior r = 0.51 (95% CL 0.34–0.76) from 66 stock assessments, resilience "Medium" **[FB]**. The CMSY prior ranges are High 0.6–1.5, Medium 0.2–0.8, Low 0.05–0.5 and Very low 0.015–0.1 **[Fr17]**; I confirmed these through several secondary sources, because the primary PDF failed TLS. With K = 1000 the model's MSY is rK/4 = 100 per season at S = 500.
- **The open-access equilibrium S\* = c/(p·q) is Gordon's** (1954) **[G54]**. In the deterministic Gordon–Schaefer model it is a *positive* equilibrium where profit is dissipated, not extinction. The "Hardin" preset collapses only because S\* is set low and noise pushes it under 2% of K. That is fine, but state the choice openly, or someone will call it rigged.
- **Slow recovery after collapse matches the evidence.** Hutchings (2000) found "very little evidence for rapid recovery from prolonged declines"; gadids such as cod recovered little 5–15 years on **[Hu00]**. Northern cod: moratorium in 1992, still below its 1980s scale when it reopened in 2024 **[CBC24]**.

### Where `DESIGN.md` misrepresents or under-uses Ostrom
1. **Switch 3 off ("imposed quota, smaller legitimacy bonus").** The evidence points to a *penalty*, not a smaller bonus: externally imposed rules with weak enforcement crowded out cooperation **[O09; Cardenas et al. 2000]**.
2. **Switch 5 off ("single flat fine… we use trivial").** Ostrom contrasts graduated sanctions with a *large* fine on a first offender: "A large monetary fine imposed on a person facing an unusual problem may produce resentment" **[O90, ch. 3]**. A faithful "off" is a harsh flat fine that drains trust, not a trivial one. Also keep switch 5 weaker than switch 4 (see the ratios above).
3. **Switch 4 is modelled only as detection probability.** The best-supported part of Ostrom's principle is *who* monitors (users, or people accountable to them). Monitoring the resource (4B in the reformulation) is also what makes switch 2's "quota tracks measured stock" possible. Either let switch 4 cover both, or make switch 2's stock estimate noisy when switch 4 is off.
4. **Switch 2 drops 2B, proportionality of costs and benefits**, which is strongly supported in both meta-analyses. The cheapest fix: when switch 2 is on, quota shares are fair, which builds trust; when it is off, shares are lopsided and the short-changed villages lose trust.
5. **Switch 8 is conditional in Ostrom** ("for CPRs that are parts of larger systems") and is the weakest-supported principle (p = 0.065). The runoff framing is a good fit; keep its effect modest.
6. **The trust model should be contingent, not hair-trigger.** Ostrom: compliance is "quasi-voluntary" and depends on others' compliance rate, and trigger-strategy models "do not describe the behavior observed in our case studies" **[O90, ch. 3]**. Keep switch 6 off as "trust drops", but let one detected cheat dent trust rather than collapse it.
7. **Framing copy.** "What separated the survivors from the collapses was institutions, which she distilled into eight design principles" is fine. Do not imply the eight are a checklist that guarantees success: Ostrom called them speculative and later "best practices", and **[Bg16]** found no single principle necessary and sufficient. The "Ostrom" preset can say "all eight best practices on" without promising a guarantee.
8. **Small facts.** Ostrom's 2009 prize was the Sveriges Riksbank Prize in Economic Sciences in Memory of Alfred Nobel, shared with Oliver Williamson **[NP]**. "Nobel 2009" is fine for UI. In the intro, "for centuries" applies to Törbel and Valencia, not to Alanya.
9. **Typo.** "Without 2 and 3 both off there is no quota at all" presumably means "**With** 2 and 3 both off…".
10. **The Hardin preset is accurate.** All-off is open access, which is exactly the commons Hardin 1968 describes and the one he conceded in 1998 was the "unmanaged" case **[H68; H98]**.
